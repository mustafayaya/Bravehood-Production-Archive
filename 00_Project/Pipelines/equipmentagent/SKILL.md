One rule above all: **every visual is skinned against the master rig** (`Assets/Characters/warrior_master_rig.blend`, 45 bones) and stitched onto the player's live bones at runtime. Anything skinned against an older/different skeleton floats or shears — there is no second skeleton.

Base meshes: `Warrior_Head` (bare head 368v), `Warrior_Torso` (armor+arms+gloves 2186v), `Warrior_Legs` (skirt+pants+boots 1924v). Tripo's factory skinning verified good (0 zero-weight verts, 0 edges >4cm across the pose battery) and kept; glove Hand weights split onto the finger bones (mesh-proportional bands, built by `Tools/Rigging/warrior/build_warrior_master.py`). ## Runbook: rig a new model / armor piece (the recurring job)

0. **FIT GATE — before anything touches the blend, understand the parts and prove the fit.** `scripts/fit_check.py --model <fbx/glb/obj> [--yaw D] [--scale S | sx,sy,sz] [--offset x,y,z]` overlays the candidate (red) on the master body (gray), renders front/left/back/persp views, and writes `fit_report.json`.
   **One model = one fit.** Fit each delivered model SEPARATELY (armor set and helmet are different models with different scales) — never merge their fitting into one transform, and export them as separate FBXs.
   **Fit means matching PROPORTIONS, not just size — use per-axis scale.** Different sets assume different body shapes; a uniform scale cannot map a tall-waisted armor figure onto the stocky warrior. Match the LANDMARK LINES per axis: armor shoulder line → body shoulder z≈0.75, waist → 0.549, ankles → 0.04 (height scale), chest depth/width → the Spine02/clavicle envelopes (r_mean table). MilitiaV2 proof: uniform 1.0 "looked seated" but every joint line was high; the correct fit was blend scale (x 0.983, y 1.085, z 0.855) — height squashed 14.5% to land the shoulder line, depth +8.5% for the warrior's barrel chest. Iterate until clip% AND the joint-line deltas are near zero; "wraps the body somewhere" is not a fit.
   **Editor hand-fit harvest (the fastest route to a proven fit):** Mustafa may drag the imported FBX into a scene and hand-scale/position the ROOT until it sits on the player — treat that as ground truth. Read the instance's localScale/localPosition via MCP and convert Unity→blend axes: `blend_scale = (unity.x, unity.z, unity.y)`, blend z-lift = unity.y offset ÷ unity globalScale. Bake that into the meshes in the blend (scale about origin AFTER the import orientation), then weight. Record the harvested numbers in the item's notes. Iterate yaw/scale/offset until the renders show the piece seated on the body (master faces **−Y**), then reuse the SAME transform when importing into the master blend for weighting. Read the report per part:
   - `class` helmet/torso/legs — sanity-check against the renders; know which part is the helmet and which is the body BEFORE choosing bones.
   - `clip_verts_pct` / `worst_clip_cm` — armor surface inside the body envelope. A seated shell reads ≤ a few % / ≤ ~2cm (surface detail); >10% = wrong scale or offset, not a weighting problem.
   - `float_verts_pct` — armor absurdly far off the body: floating shell, wrong class, or wrong offset.
   - `bones_spanned` — which bone envelopes the part actually wraps = the bones that must deform it. Compare with `suggested_mask` (the slot's proven deform set). A "helmet" spanning clavicles is sitting too low or isn't a helmet.
   Cross-check against `references/master_proportions.json` (per-bone surface envelopes + landmarks). Wider/bulkier armor is FINE — what matters is that the piece wraps the skeleton's joints at the right heights so bone heat binds each region to the right bone: helmet interior clears the head sphere (r 0.134), torso shoulder line at z≈0.75, wrists at z≈0.44 x≈±0.25, waist seam near z 0.549, ankles z≈0.04. If a joint of the armor (elbow crease, knee plate) sits >3–4cm from the rig's joint, fix the transform — or accept visible hinging at the wrong height forever.
1. **Into the blend**: import the Tripo mesh with the fit-gate transform applied (never let Tripo rig equipment against the master — its skeletons drift per generation). Parent-clear (keep transform) → transform-apply. **Visually confirm overlap with the character** — Tripo orientations vary ±90°; a transfer done misaligned is garbage, rotating after the fact does not fix it. Decimate toward existing part budgets (old parts are 0.5–2k verts, Armor B pieces ~12k).
2. **Weighting — USE BONE HEAT, NOT DATA TRANSFER.** `bpy.ops.object.parent_set(type='ARMATURE_AUTO')` (mesh + armature selected, armature active). Blender's bone-heat diffusion travels *through the mesh volume*, so it cannot bleed hip weights onto a hand hanging 5cm away in the air — the exact failure that makes Data Transfer unusable on arms-at-sides Tripo figures. On the Militia set it scored **0 stretched faces** across the full pose battery, versus 165+ for every Data-Transfer-plus-repairs attempt. Verify with the pose battery (below) and expect literally zero. Bone heat leaves disconnected islands (rivets/studs) unweighted → run the small-island rebind (copy weights from the nearest big-island vertex) right after, or studs freeze in mid-air. Data Transfer stays only as a fallback if bone heat errors out ("failed to find solution for one or more bones").
   **Bone heat binds to whatever bone is near, so MASK the bone set per mesh** (`bone.use_deform = False` before the call, restore after). Arms-at-sides figures put the hands right beside the skirt → without a mask ~40% of skirt verts bind to `L/R_Hand` and the skirt swings with the sword arm. The head bone reaches through the neck hole → the mail collar binds 100% to `Head` and spins when the player looks around. Working masks: **Legs** = Hip/Pelvis/Waist/Spine01 + leg chain; **Torso** = Hip/Pelvis/Waist/Spine01-02 + NeckTwist01/02 + arm chain incl. `L/R_Fingers1-2` for glove pieces (or split Hand bands after, per the finger-grip recipe), **Head excluded** (neck twists are fine — a collar following the neck slightly is correct).
   **Bone heat FAILS SILENTLY on a restricted bone set** — it prints only a Warning and leaves *every* vertex at zero weight. ALWAYS count zero-weight verts right after the call. The legs mesh fails with a lower-body-only mask; its recipe is full-bone heat → strip arm+head chains → renormalize → rebuild emptied verts from edge neighbours.
   **Never leave a vertex unweighted.** It freezes at bind pose while its neighbours move, producing exactly the dark spikes/shards that look like "broken faces" — the artifact I twice misdiagnosed as bad geometry and "fixed" by deleting faces. Final pass on every mesh: any vert with total weight <0.001 copies the weights of its nearest weighted vert (kdtree).
   **Shoulder caps**: heat binds the whole cap to `Clavicle` (~100%/0% vs Upperarm) because the clavicle tail sits at the shoulder joint — the pauldron then stays put while the sleeve swings, which reads as "shoulder joints positioned wrong". Fix with an explicit gradient along the clavicle axis: u = projection of the vert onto head→tail, hand `0.80*smoothstep((u-0.45)/0.55)` of the clavicle weight over to `Upperarm` (0.85 beyond the joint).
   **Separated clothable parts (skirt split from the torso for cloth-sim readiness) MUST share EXACT weights at the connection ring** — a ramped blend still leaves centimetres of drift and the junction opens a visible gap in game (MilitiaV2 belt/skirt gap, 2026-08-25). Rule: every vert of the child piece's connection ring (top ~3cm band) COPIES the weights of the nearest parent-piece rim vert verbatim, then fades its own weighting back in over the next ~5cm. Same principle as vertex-painting the junction to identical values. Verify with the posed drift measure — the ring itself must read ~0cm.
   **Seams BETWEEN pieces (torso hem vs legs/belt) need weight continuity, or the character opens a see-through slit at the waist.** Each piece is weighted independently, so the torso hem can land on `Spine02` while the legs' top rim lands on `Spine01` — any spine bend shears them apart, and the overlap is thin (Militia: torso core stops at z 0.56, legs core reaches 0.59 = 3cm). Fix, weights-only: for torso verts below z 0.68 whose top bone is a core bone (never arm/hand geometry), blend their weights toward those of the nearest legs vertex, ramping to 100% at z 0.60. Measure it properly — pair each rim vert with its nearest vertex on the other mesh at rest, pose the spine hard, and report how much that distance grows; drift must stay well under the overlap depth (Militia went 3.6cm → 0cm).
   **Any hard weight ban creates a seam.** Banning arm weights on the torso's mail hem left a discontinuity at the armpit (5-10× stretch). Smooth the seam band afterwards (`vertex_group_smooth` masked to z 0.53-0.68, radius 0.08-0.22) — at the armpit a gradient is correct; never extend such smoothing down into the rigid hand region.
3. **Clean arm bleed** (mandatory for anything covering the hips/legs): `scripts/fix_arm_bleed.py --meshes <names>`. Tripo figures pose arms-at-sides, so hand/forearm weights bleed onto hip/skirt/thigh plates → spikes that follow the sword arm in game. The script keeps lower-arm weights only inside big edge-connected arm islands (real arm shells ~1500–2000 verts; bleed pockets ≤250) and rebuilds stripped verts from neighbors. **Distance-based cleanup fails** — skirt hems sit within 7cm of the hanging hands. Legs meshes get zero arm weights.
   **⚠ Steps 3 and the "webbing" saga below are LEGACY Data-Transfer repair work — with bone heat you need NONE of it.** Hard-won correction (2026-08-20): the "Tripo welds the hands to the hips" diagnosis was WRONG. A purely geometric check (faces spanning 5cm→9cm of hand-bone distance) found **zero** bridge faces in the source mesh. Every "webbing" face deleted across many passes was a legitimate sleeve/armpit face condemned by weight-family tests that were themselves reading Data-Transfer's bad weights — which is why each cut spawned new holes and new artifacts. **Never delete geometry to fix a deformation bug until a weight method that cannot bleed (bone heat) has been tried and still fails.** The user's mesh was pristine the whole time. Legacy notes kept only for Data-Transfer fallback:
   **Tripo hand-hip webbing (Militia torso): arms-at-sides figures come with junk faces WELDING the inner hands to the hip/hem surface** — half the hand inherits thigh/spine weights and no weight fix can help: the welded faces tear into skin-colored cones the moment the arm moves. Fix order: (1) DELETE the bridging faces (faces mixing arm-chain and body top-weights in the wrist-hip zone), (2) rebind the hand region — flood-grow from verts within 6cm of the hand-bone segment, solid `Hand 1.0` below the wrist crease, then a ~6cm analytic blend band Hand→ForearmTwist02→Forearm (a HARD boundary tears; twist02 matches the TwistBoneDriver roll), (3) iterate with the pose-tear diagnostic: pose arms/wrists in bpy, flag verts whose edges stretch >4cm vs rest, rebind flagged body-weighted verts, repeat until only wrist-band stretch remains. Weight-vis renders (color faces by top bone) beat guessing filters. (4) leftover ribbon webs: delete faces whose POSED AREA grows >2.5× rest area AND whose corners mix arm/body families — surgical and safe. Run the stretch test as a POSE BATTERY (hand ±60-70° on all three axes, forearm 70°, combined swings — a single mild pose misses webs that only tear at game-animation extremes). (5) **the deepest layer: SUB-WEIGHT contamination** — verts can be arm-dominant on top yet carry 30-45% thigh/spine underneath; invisible to every top-bone test and the true cause of 10-30× face stretch on "pure" faces. Fix: below the armpit line (z<0.62, where no legit arm↔body blend exists) force every vert fully to its dominant family (drop cross-family groups, renormalize) — weights only, delete nothing. Do this BEFORE judging battery results. Same-family faces that stretch are wrist-band deformation: leave them — and NEVER "fix" them by masked vertex_group_smooth (smoothing bleeds across the deliberate rigid hand boundary and multiplies the tearing; if the wrist degrades, re-run the hand flood-grow rebind, which is deterministic and restores it exactly). **NEVER do a zone-wide purify-families-then-delete-mixed-faces loop**: purify creates artificial family boundaries mid-surface and the next delete pass blows holes through legit sleeves/armpits (this destroyed a torso once — full rebuild required). Face-delete criteria must be per-face evidence (mixed weld tops at the ORIGINAL state, or posed-area stretch), never derived from weights you just rewrote. Ghost note: `delete(type='ONLY_FACE')` keeps edges — the tear diagnostic reports them forever after; they're invisible, filter to faces-only or ignore.
   **Low-poly trap (Militia, ~2k-vert pieces): READ THE SUMMARY.** Arm islands on small sets are ~350 verts — below the default `--big-island 400` — so the script strips the REAL arms (symptom: `islands` list tops out below 400, huge `nearest_fallback`). Re-transfer that mesh's weights and re-run with `--big-island 250`. Bare hands are their OWN small islands (skin ≠ sleeve): even at 250 they get stripped and fall back to thigh/upperarm — repair with a masked DataTransfer (mask = verts within 7cm of lower-arm bones whose top weight isn't arm-chain) from the donor. Then rebind every remaining tiny island (<40 verts: rivets/rings) by copying weights from the nearest big-island vertex, else studs float when posed. Finish with a bpy pose-test render (rotate Upperarm/Thigh/Calf in pose mode) BEFORE export.
4. **Export**: `scripts/export_equipment.py --meshes <names> --out Assets/Characters/Equipment/<Set>.fbx` (defaults to the warrior master; pass `--blend .../knight_master_rig.blend` for legacy sets). One FBX per delivered model (armor set ≠ helmet). **Meshes MUST be parented to the Armature in the blend before export** — a parentless skinned mesh gets its own FBX axis conversion while the armature carries 270°X, and the piece renders pitched 90° in Unity (MilitiaV2 helmet, 2026-08-25); the export script now enforces this. Non-negotiable flags are baked in — especially `apply_scale_options='FBX_SCALE_ALL'` (without it the Armature node carries 100× scale and stitched plates render 100× too small around each bone).
5. **Unity importer**: `globalScale=1.5941` for warrior-master exports / 1.8795 for legacy knight-blend exports (blend metric → hip 0.780 world), `materialImportMode=None`, rig **Generic/NoAvatar — NEVER rig None** (None silently strips all SkinnedMeshRenderers; equips no-op with no error). **Bravehood art style geometry (flat-shaded): Normals=Calculate, Normals Mode=Area And Angle Weighted, Smoothness Source=None** (the flat-shade key — kills smoothing groups for the faceted look), Weld Vertices on, Tangents=CalculateMikk, Import Vertex Colors on. `Assets/Editor/BravehoodModelDefaults.cs` applies these automatically on FIRST import of anything under `Assets/Characters/` (importSettingsMissing only, so later Inspector tweaks are never fought).
6. **Verify import**: bone chains lossyScale 1, Hip world y = 0.780, SMR bones=45 (legacy cloth cape stays 41).
7. **Wire**: material (URP Lit + the model's basecolor/normal; normal map importer type must be NormalMap), `EquipmentItem` asset in `Assets/Core/Data/Equipment/`, add to an `EquipmentDebugSwapper` set.
8. **Play-test BOTH swap directions and MOTION**: idle hides weight bugs — verify in a running pose (arms swing past hips) and mid-attack. Use `scripts/diagnose_weights.py` when something looks off.

## Runtime system (Assets/Core/Scripts/Gameplay/Equipment/, namespace `Bravehood.Equipment`)

- **`EquipmentSlot`** — `Helmet, Torso, Legs, Weapon`.
- **`EquipmentItem`** (SO, *Bravehood ▸ Equipment Item*) — `slot`, `modelSource` (rigged FBX root), `meshObjectNames` (child SMRs to use; empty = all), `overrideMaterial`, `hideHead` (closed helms / torsos with integrated helmet), Weapon slot: `attachBone` + local offset (rigid parent, no skinning).
- **Capes belong to the Torso item** (`capeMode`): `None` / `NativeCape` (the player's tuned `cloth` child — never rebuild it) / `CustomPrefab` (cloth-sim cape prefab; SMR bones re-targeted by name KEEPING the component so Cloth tuning survives; empty `Cloth.capsuleColliders` get the rig's `ClothCol_*` capsules). Torso swap swaps the cape; no torso = no cape. New capes follow [[cape-cloth-setup]]: zero selfCollision*, zero externalAcceleration, pin the shoulder region.
- **`EquipmentManager`** (on Player root in `Assets/Core/Prefabs/Gameplay/Player.prefab`) — bone map by **name** from `rigRoot` (`Root` under `Armature`). Spawns `EQ_<slot>_<mesh>` children with `updateWhenOffscreen=true` (copied localBounds are garbage under unit-scale nodes). Awake: disables `nativeArmorRenderers` (baked-in tripo_part_6/10/14/16) and sweeps orphaned `EQ_*` children (domain reload mid-play wipes the tracking dicts). `headRenderer` (tripo_part_18) auto-hides per `hideHead`.
- **`EquipmentDebugSwapper`** (same GO) — keys **1..9** (new Input System) apply `sets[i]`. Set 1 = Old Knight (helmet part_14+16, gambeson part_10, boots part_6 from `OldArmor_SetA.fbx`), Set 2 = Armor Set B (Chest_B `hideHead` + Legs_B from `Armor_Set_B.fbx`), Set 3 = Militia T1 (Militia_Helmet open-face [head stays visible] + Militia_Torso [incl. bare hands] + Militia_Legs from `Militia_Set.fbx`; blend transforms: set yaw 90° scale 0.83 off (0.105,0.012,0), helmet scale 0.32 off (0.04,0.012,0.70)).
- **`EquipmentDebugGUI`** (same GO) — on-screen IMGUI panel, toggle **F9**: one column per slot (Helmet/Torso/Legs/Weapon) with None + every item in its `items` array, so helmets and armors swap independently. Opening it frees the cursor (`PlayerInputHandler` locks it in Awake) and suspends gameplay input by disabling every enabled action map on the assets referenced by the player's components — otherwise mousing to a button also swings the camera and clicks land as attacks; closing restores the exact previous lock state and re-enables only the maps it disabled. Starts closed (`visible = false`). New items must be added to BOTH the swapper set and `EquipmentDebugGUI.items`.
- **Partial cloth on stitched pieces** (`EquipmentItem.simulateCloth`; Militia helmet aventail + skirt): manager adds a `Cloth` to the spawned SMR — verts above `clothAnchorBone`.y + `clothPinOffset` stay fully skinned (maxDistance 0), below it freedom ramps over `clothFalloff` up to `clothMaxDistance`. **Bravehood cloth standard** in `SetupPartialCloth` (Mustafa, 2026-08-25 — replaced the old heavy-mail 0.7-damping tuning): solver 240Hz, **stretch 0.2 / bend 0.6, damping 0.15** (lively AAA drape — bend runs FIRMER than the authored 0.2: on low-poly game cloth a soft bend folds the surface at TRIANGLE scale and flat shading turns every dihedral crease into a flashing facet, the MilitiaV2 'glitchy skirt'; 0.6 keeps folds at panel scale while stretch 0.2 supplies the sway), **useTethers on, enableContinuousCollision on, useVirtualParticles 1**, collisionMassScale 0, friction 0.6, worldVelocity/AccelerationScale 0 (kills locomotion-pumped jitter), zero selfCollision/externalAcceleration. **Cloth-simulated meshes render with `Bravehood/ClothFlatShaded`** (Assets/Core/Shaders — double-sided, flat facet normals via _Flatness, _BaseMap/_BaseColor/_BackfaceTint; set it on the piece's material with **_Smoothness 0** — specular pings on moving facets read as glitches; cloth deformation + the flat art style make URP Lit's baked normals wrong). Colliders: `ClothCol_Spine/Hip/Chest/L_Thigh/R_Thigh` trigger capsules on the rig (Chest r=0.19 matches the ARMOR surface, not the body — undersized capsules read as "hem flying"). Tuned values — aventail: pin 0.12/falloff 0.25/max 0.15 anchored to Head (pin the WHOLE collar ring or it sags open a seam under the dome). **Island gate before enabling cloth on any piece**: count edge-connected islands and their z-bands (Tools/Rigging/skirt_islands.py pattern). A fully-free island (no pinned verts) sags to its maxDistance limit instantly — so every island that is not part of the main shell must lie ABOVE the pin line (stays skinned) or be welded in. MilitiaV2 skirt passed: one 626-vert shell spans the whole drape + 22 rivet islands all in the pinned stud band (pin `Hip` −0.038, falloff 0.20, max 0.09). The knight-era Militia skirt FAILED this gate (layered shells) and shipped skinned — that remains the fallback. **Traps**: cloth verts live in the SMR's ROOT-BONE space (classify via `rootBone.TransformPoint` against the LIVE player bone); `clothMeshName` restricts cloth to one mesh of a multi-mesh item — cloth-skirts must be SPLIT into their own mesh in the blend (region-select faces, `bpy.ops.mesh.separate`) or boots/pants inherit freedom; separate mesh islands simulate independently (hairline cracks under motion — weld if visible).
- **Weapon grip auto-fit** (`EquipmentManager.weaponGrip` / `gripBone` / `autoFitGrip`) — every Tripo set models its hands in a different spot relative to `R_Hand`, so one authored sword offset fits one armour and the weapon visibly floats for all the others (Militia's palm sits ~9cm from the old knight's gauntlet). The manager measures each equipped piece's skinned palm — centroid of vertices weighted >0.5 to `gripBone`, pushed through `mesh.bindposes[bone]` into hand-bone space, cached per mesh — and keeps the weapon at the same position *relative to that palm*. The first piece equipped in `Start` (the default loadout) defines the authored relationship, so the prefab's hand-tuned offset stays exactly as authored for that set and every later armour inherits it automatically; no per-armour tuning. Needs **Read/Write Enabled** on the equipment FBX importers (guarded — logs a warning and falls back to the fixed offset if off). `EquipmentItem.gripPositionOffset/gripEulerOffset` are optional per-piece nudges added on top (rotation is not auto-fitted). **Harvesting a hand-tune (proven MilitiaV2 2026-08-25)**: Mustafa positions the live `Sword` transform in play mode with the armor worn; capture it BEFORE play stops, then per the ApplyGrip math: `gripPositionOffset = userLocalPos − (TryPalmCentroid() + _gripFromPalm)` and `gripEulerOffset = (Inverse(_gripBaseLocalRot) * userLocalRot).eulerAngles` (as signed angles), written to the hands-supplying item (the Torso). Verify by swapping away and back — the manager must reproduce the captured transform exactly. MilitiaV2 torso: pos (−0.0075, −0.0534, 0.0399), euler (6.18, −19.26, −6.83).
- **`TwistBoneDriver`** (Assets/Core/Scripts/Gameplay/, on Player root) — **humanoid animation never drives twist bones**; tight armor skinned to them shears at the wrist when the hand rolls (baggy sleeves hid this for months). LateUpdate swing-twist decomposition of the source bone's roll, distributed cumulatively down the chain. Wired: `L/R_Hand` → ForearmTwist01 (0.30) / ForearmTwist02 (0.65). Shearing at shoulders/thighs = add a chain the same way. Paused-mode test: force `hand.localRotation = rest * AngleAxis(55°, axis)`, `drv.SendMessage("LateUpdate")` → twist01 16.5°, twist02 +19° local.

## Player-rig / avatar traps (check in this order when the character collapses, crouches, faces wrong, or armor floats)

- **Armor floats at shoulders** → piece was skinned against a stale skeleton; re-export from the CURRENT master blend. (Native head part_18 & cape keep old bindposes deliberately — their bones moved <1cm.)
- **Prefab looks right, scene player broken** → PlayKit.prefab instance overrides mask Player.prefab bone edits (a saved pose can live in ~175 `m_Local*` overrides). Fix: `GetPropertyModifications` on the instance root, drop `m_Local*` mods targeting bone Transforms, `SetPropertyModifications`.
- **Wrong player** → the scene has TWO instances: `PlayKit/Player` (real, camera-followed) and `PlayKit/Player (1)` (test dummy; `FindFirstObjectByType` returns it). Select by name. Both receive input and swapper keys.
- **Silent retarget garbage** → humanoid avatar skeleton chain must match the scene hierarchy node-for-node (ParentNode was deleted from Player.prefab for this).
- **Avatar rebuild recipe** (SerializedObject on the importer — the C# `humanDescription` setter silently no-ops; write `m_HumanDescription.m_Human` AND `m_Skeleton` in ONE pass, then SaveAndReimport):
  - `m_Human` = the proven mapping copied from the old medieval_knight avatar (incl. quirky `Hips→Root`); auto-mapping assigns calf/forearm twists as Foot/Hand.
  - `m_Skeleton` = OLD avatar's entries for every unchanged bone — **this defines height AND facing** (raw FBX Root rot (0,0,270°) = character yawed 90° from controller; wrong-mapping skeleton = Hip 0.569 → humanScale 0.632 → permanent crouch). NEW FBX TRS only for intentionally moved bones (the rebuilt shoulder chain) and the Armature wrapper.
  - Sanity probes: `Animator.humanScale` ≈ 0.85–0.88; facing = L_Foot→L_ToeBase horizontal vector vs `transform.forward` ≈ 0°.

## Verify checklist after any equipment change

- Play mode: `runInBackground=true` first call; call `ApplySet(i)` on the **named** Player's swapper.
- `EQ_*` bounds sane (not 0.01, not offset); head visibility follows `hideHead`; cape follows torso.
- Test in MOTION (run + attack), not just idle; check hip/skirt plates and wrists specifically.
- `Debug.LogWarning` from execute_code-triggered equips does NOT show in `read_console` — inspect state directly.
- After any session touching the player: grep PlayKit.prefab/Player.prefab for `m_Enabled: 0` regressions.

## Known open issues / quality ceiling
